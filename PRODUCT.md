# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Stack

delegated: a dependency-free static HTML, CSS, and JavaScript site was selected for fast, reliable publishing as a portfolio presentation.

## Users

Prospective clients planning a private countryside home. They are comparing architects before committing to an introductory conversation and need to understand the studio's point of view and project approach.

## Product Purpose

«ТИШЬ» is a concept architecture studio site. It presents three illustrative home concepts and gives a visitor a clear, low-friction way to begin a project conversation.

## Positioning

The studio starts from a family's habits and a specific site, then lets landscape, material and daily rhythm shape the house.

## Operating Context

Visitors browse on a phone or desktop, usually early in their search. They need an immediate visual sense of the studio and a concise explanation of the process before they share contact details.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- A single-page responsive portfolio with project details, mobile navigation, and a contact form.
- Project imagery and names are illustrative concepts, not completed commissions.
- The contact form validates locally and clearly states that delivery is a demonstration until a backend is connected.
- The site must be keyboard-friendly, readable, and honor reduced-motion preferences.

## Brand Commitments

- Name: «ТИШЬ».
- Voice: calm, precise, unpretentious Russian.
- Materials: weathered timber, stone, glass, wet earth, reeds and soft daylight.

## Evidence on Hand

- Three generated architectural images in `assets/`; they are illustrative concept imagery.
- No real contacts, testimonials, awards, client logos, construction statistics, or delivered projects may be asserted.

## Product Principles

1. Let the architecture and landscape carry attention before interface chrome.
2. Name a useful next step clearly and without sales pressure.
3. Keep every claim demonstrably conceptual when it is not supplied as a fact.
4. Make restraint feel deliberate through editorial spacing and type.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Use semantic landmarks, labeled inputs, strong visible focus, readable contrast, responsive type, and reduced-motion behavior.
